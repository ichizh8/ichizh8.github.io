A place to keep my bearings

I started chizh.us because living inside a permanent stream of AI announcements is a bad way to understand what any of it is doing to us.

Why I keep it

My own perception changes as I use these systems. Tasks that felt strange become routine. Interfaces begin to shape what I notice, what I trust, and what I consider good work. The shifts are often too gradual to see while they are happening.

I write them down because memory is a generous editor. It smooths over uncertainty, removes wrong turns, and makes every conclusion look inevitable. A written argument is less flattering and much more useful.

Three lenses

Research tells me what has actually been measured, in which setting, and with what limits. Practice tests whether an idea survives contact with real products, teams, incentives, and constraints. Making things exposes possibilities that papers and meetings have not named yet.

Science can lag. Practice can overfit. Creativity can turn into theater with alarming speed. I want the friction between them, because each catches a different kind of nonsense.

What I keep returning to

I keep returning to what happens to judgment when generation becomes cheap; where delegation ends and responsibility starts; how interfaces teach us to trust, comply, or stop noticing; and what prolonged use does to attention, ambition, taste, and the feeling that a piece of work is still yours.

These are recurring questions, not a permanent taxonomy. The vocabulary should change when the work changes. A neat taxonomy too early is usually just decoration.

How I publish

I separate research findings from my own judgments and put citations next to factual claims. I try not to promote one study into a law of human nature. When the evidence is weak, I say so. When I change my mind, the earlier argument stays part of the record.

I publish when a question has earned an argument. The calendar will cope.

Not a funnel

This is not an AI news digest, prompt collection, tool ranking, productivity sermon, or personal-brand content machine. The internet already has enough of those, including several that now claim to be autonomous.

Atrum.dev is my separate studio. It may appear when the work produces a useful case, under the same evidence rules as anything else. The journal is not its lead-generation annex.

Mostly, I keep this journal for myself. It is public because thinking becomes more honest when someone else can inspect the work.

Colophon

The site is static Astro with self-hosted Newsreader and a system monospace register. It has no analytics, cookies, trackers, embeds, or third-party font requests. The one readable article remains an internal preview, not a published piece.
